Read this first. Making the signal more explicit did not help. The low-difficulty rows (regime stated almost outright) score 73.3%, below the medium rows (80.0%) and about the same as the hard set (76.9% hard / 68.0% very hard). The errors are the same two as on the hard set, and they happen with high confidence:
  • Reg S-ID collapses (F1 0.38). Only 7 of 30 identity-theft rows are labelled S-ID; 12 go to Reg S-P and 8 to FINRA 4530. Even “I think someone has taken over my account” is read as a privacy matter or a complaint.
  • Short, direct Reg BI recommendations are read as FINRA 2210. “Buy the Copperleaf Dividend Fund. For your situation, it's the right call.” is predicted 2210 at 0.99. The training Reg BI rows are long and hedged, so the model learned that style, and a blunt recommendation looks like promissory 2210 language to it.
  • Violations predicted compliant: 10 of 180, fewer than on the hard set (22 of 270), so the clean/violation boundary is not the main problem; it's which regime.

The linear probe is slightly worse here (0.720 vs 0.748) and better on the hard set; the CIs overlap both times. Together the two OOD runs say the model keys on the surface form of the training templates more than on the concepts, so the fix is training data with more varied phrasing (developer guide, P1 #5), not a harder test.
